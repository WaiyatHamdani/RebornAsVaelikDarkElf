Chapter 3: The Coming Age of the Noble Warrior

Vaelik stood at the balcony's edge, the wind curling through his silver hair like a whisper from the future. Below him, the obsidian towers of House Vael’thryn glimmered under a velvet sky scattered with starlight. He was ten now—old enough to undergo the Rite of the Noble Warrior, a sacred test passed down through generations of dark elf nobility. And yet, instead of anticipation, a cold knot of tension coiled in his gut.

"You're up early," came a soft voice behind him.

Lysira. She approached with her usual grace, her dark robes brushing silently against the marble floor. Her crimson eyes gleamed in the half-light.

"I couldn't sleep," Vaelik murmured. "Too much to think about."

Lysira joined him at the balcony, her gaze sweeping across the vast capital of the Underdark kingdom. "Understandable. The Rite is no small thing—even for a child who has already awakened the Devourer."

Vaelik didn’t answer. He was thinking about the maps—the endless scrolls and tomes filled with scribbled borders and ancient names. For the past month, his days had been consumed not just with sword drills or shadow conjurations, but lessons in the world beyond.

High above their dark kingdom, the surface world teemed with life—kingdoms, clans, and secrets. Knowledge of these lands was the foundation of any true noble warrior.

His lessons began with the Arc-Mage Thandor, a skeletal figure with hollow cheeks and sunken eyes who spoke of magic as though it were both scripture and science. He unveiled illusions of fire-torn landscapes and frost-covered peaks, outlining the unstable borders between warring nations.

"Magic is power," Thandor had rasped. "But knowledge is the chain that binds it. Without understanding the balance of the world, your strength will only destroy you."

Next came the Scholar Helyra, a sharp-eyed woman with ink-stained fingers who forced Vaelik to memorize the Great Map of the Surface. She tapped the scroll with her feathered quill as she spoke.

"The Kublai Clans are warriors born—barbarians to some, yes, but their instincts are honed like blades. They live close to the lost kingdom to the north, where muscle and fury outweigh strategy. Do not underestimate them."

She shifted her quill.

"To the west lies the Orc Encampment. Strong, proud, tribal. Once war-mongers, now scattered and hardened. Many see them as monsters, but they value strength and honor."

Her quill danced again.

"The Gale Kingdom. A realm of humans—structured and politically cautious. They maintain an uneasy peace, but their knights are disciplined and well-trained."

Then she frowned and pointed farther south.

"The Naratas Empire—also human—but unlike Gale, they thrive on conquest. War defines them. They respect only results."

Finally, she tapped a blank spot on the map.

"The Lost Kingdom," she whispered. "Dwarves. No one has seen the gates in centuries. They live beneath the surface, carving their homes deeper than light dares reach. If their kingdom still lives, its strength is untouched by time."

Vaelik soaked in every word. Every contour of the map. Every name and symbol.

Because this was more than memorization.

It was preparation.

At Sword Academy, Master Kael doubled the intensity of training. No longer did he allow mistakes. Vaelik was pushed to his limits—forced to spar not just with illusions, but with real challengers: animated constructs, summoned monsters, and senior students.

"Your enemy will not hesitate," Kael growled. "You may only Devour twelve skills. Choose wisely, but fight fiercely. The world will not give you second chances."

Vaelik’s Devour count remained at one—Perception-Eyes—but he had begun to see how valuable it truly was. He could read the auras of those around him, glimpsing the shimmer of skill or the void of weakness. He began seeing patterns in combat—preempting moves, recognizing potential threats.

Then came the Catsith.

A diplomatic envoy had arrived from the southern coasts—demi-humans with feline ears and sharp golden eyes. Agile, curious, and quick-witted, they were emissaries from a neutral trade city, curious about the awakening of the Devourer.

Vaelik had sparred with Yari, a girl his age with razor-sharp claws and even sharper instincts.

She didn’t speak much. She just fought.

And smiled.

He lost that match.

But he learned.

"You hesitate before striking," she had whispered after, helping him off the ground. "You think too much. The world moves faster than thoughts."

Vaelik took the lesson to heart. He no longer fought with his mind alone. He let instinct guide his blade.

After Vaelik turned ten, the day of the Rite dawned with no fanfare.

Vaelik was dressed in black armor threaded with violet runes, a blade strapped across his back, and a shadow gem around his neck—given to him by Malriya to boost his affinity in times of crisis.

The elders of House Vael’thryn watched from the high walls as he descended the spiral path out of the city. No one wished him luck.

It wasn’t tradition.

His destination was a forest near the sun-cracked surface, filled with gnarled trees and strange twilight light. The werewolf had been sighted days ago—large, fast, feral.

He found it by nightfall.

It was drinking from a creek, its reflection warped in the water. Vaelik crept silently, shadow wrapping around his form like a cloak. He activated Perception-Eyes.

And saw it.

The werewolf shimmered with one distinct aura:

!! Absolute Severance !!

This skill can cut anything—making blocking useless. This is an amazing skill to have.

He drew his blade and charged, channeling shadow into his limbs for speed. The werewolf howled leapt to meet him—and the battle began.

Steel clashed against claw. Flesh tore. Blood spilled.

Vaelik screamed—not in fear, but in focus. He let the pain sharpen him. He moved with purpose, reading every twitch of the beast’s muscle, every flicker of skill aura.

He waited.

Then, when the werewolf lunged for a final, fatal strike—he called to the darkness.

"Devour."

Black tendrils burst forth, binding the beast mid-air.

	Devour initiated.
	Target possesses skill: Absolute Severance.
	Skill assimilated.
	Note: Current skill count: 2/12.

The beast crashed to the earth—and Vaelik stood victorious.

He tested Absolute Severance on the werewolf’s corpse and beheaded it cleanly.

When he returned, bloodied and pale, the elders said nothing—because they saw the head of the beast being carried by young Vaelik.

After seeing that, they only bowed.

The ten-year-old dark elf had become a warrior.

And far beyond the Underdark, in the Kublai north, the winds carried whispers of a child born of shadow.

The world had begun to take notice.
